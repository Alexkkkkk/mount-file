# Native exFAT: two-FAT / TexFAT limitation

The native reader and writer accept only bounded, validated **one-FAT** exFAT volumes (`NumberOfFats=1`, `ActiveFat=0`). A volume declaring `NumberOfFats=2` is refused for native browsing and all native mutations, regardless of `ActiveFat`, FAT contents, or whether its root directory contains zero, one, or both allocation bitmaps. This is a deliberate `unsupported_profile` refusal before the reader traverses root metadata or the writer creates a stage or changes the image.

Per the [Microsoft exFAT specification](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/win32/fileio/exfat-specification), sections 3.1.16 and 7.1, two FATs are for TexFAT and require two allocation bitmap entries with BitmapIdentifier 0 and 1. The earlier interpretation that two FATs were ordinary mirrors sharing one bitmap was incorrect. Mirroring FAT writes is not a TexFAT transaction or recovery protocol; no TexFAT browsing, writes, proof of consistency, or recovery support is claimed here.

`probe_exfat_boot_region` is a **geometry/boot-region probe only**: it may return a profile with two FATs without validating TexFAT allocation metadata or authorizing native access. It checks both boot-region checksums and agreement on geometry, including `NumberOfFats`; status fields such as `VolumeFlags` and `PercentInUse` may be stale in the backup copy. Native VFS and `UniversalFSRW` reject a recognized two-FAT profile rather than falling back after the native refusal.

For the supported one-FAT profile, the reader rejects dirty or media-failure volumes but allows `ClearToZero` read-only. The writer also refuses `ClearToZero` because it does not implement its pre-write handling; reserved `VolumeFlags` bits are rejected by the probe. The reader never repairs or clears these flags.

File streams honor `NoFatChain`. Contiguous streams are read without interpreting FAT entries; ordinary chains reject invalid links and cycles. The allocation bitmap is authoritative for stream allocation; free-cluster allocation also requires a zero FAT entry. Entry sets, name hashes, up-case mapping, stream lengths, and allocation ownership are checked within the bounded writer profile. Writes are staged on a complete image copy, read back, and committed only after a full source-image identity check. Staging does not supply a power-loss recovery guarantee.
